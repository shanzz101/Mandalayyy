Welcome to Mandalay

1) Mandalay, the second-largest city in Myanmar, lies on the eastern bank of the Ayeyarwady River about 630 km north of Yangon. Founded in 1857 by King Mindon, it was the last royal capital of the Burmese kings and remains the spiritual and cultural center of the nation. The city takes its name from Mandalay Hill, a sacred site that offers panoramic views of the city and is dotted with ancient pagodas and monasteries.

2) At the heart of Mandalay stands the Royal Palace, originally built during the Konbaung Dynasty and surrounded by an impressive moat and fortified walls. Other iconic landmarks include the Maha Myat Muni Pagoda, home to one of Myanmar’s most revered Buddha images; the Kuthodaw Pagoda, known as the World’s Largest Book with 729 marble slabs inscribed with Buddhist scriptures; and the U Bein Bridge in nearby Amarapura, the world’s oldest and longest teakwood bridge.

3) Mandalay is also the nation’s hub for traditional arts and crafts—gold-leaf making, wood carving, silk weaving, and marble sculpting thrive here. The city’s vibrant markets, like Zegyo Market, offer a glimpse into local life and handmade treasures, while its street food scene reflects Myanmar’s diverse ethnic influences.

4) Surrounded by ancient royal capitals such as Ava, Sagaing, and Amarapura, and with hill stations like Pyin Oo Lwin nearby, Mandalay serves as the perfect base to explore Myanmar’s historical heartland.
